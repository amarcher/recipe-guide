Weekly planner — how this thing is meant to be used

What "Regenerate skeleton" and "Regenerate candidates" actually do, and where the rest of the week fits.

The planner is a new layer on top of the existing recipe-execution app. It answers a different question: what are we eating this week and what do we need to buy? — rather than how do I cook this recipe right now?

11 slides. Arrow keys, or click right/left on the screen to navigate.

The two buttons currently on the plan page — Regenerate skeleton and Regenerate candidates — trigger stages 2 and 3. Stage 1 (intake) isn't wired yet; we've seeded a realistic intake to demo everything downstream.

Stage 1 · Intake

A Sunday-night conversation, not a form

A lightweight chat with the agent produces a structured PlanIntake: week mood (use-up / explore / survival), pantry items, aspirations, kid rules (reliable hits + hard nos), cook-time limits, which meals to cover.

Agent: "Hey — what's the vibe this week?" You: "We've got a pound of ground turkey to use. Kids need dinners + breakfasts. Adults lunches Mon-Fri. One explore slot would be nice." Agent: "Got it. Any aspirations lingering?" You: "I've been curious about shakshuka." Agent: "Kids' hard-nos still fish and mushrooms?" You: "Yep." Agent: [calls signal_intake_complete]

Output is a structured object the downstream stages consume. This UI is still to build — next milestone. Today the planner runs off a seeded intake with the exact same shape.

Stage 2 · Skeleton

The thesis for the week — no recipes yet

One LLM call. Input: your intake + recent cooking history. Output: hero ingredients (what we'll reuse across slots), themes (how the week hangs together), a constraint acknowledgment, and a narrative rationale.

heroIngredients: - ground turkey → DINNER x2 (must use) - spinach → DINNER x1, LUNCH x1 - eggs → DINNER x1 (shakshuka), BREAKFAST x2 themes: - Clear the fridge: turkey + spinach lead the week - Mediterranean weeknights for adults, one shakshuka explore rationale: "You said turkey and spinach are the pressure points, so they lead. The shakshuka curiosity gets its one explore slot and uses the eggs and spinach you already have..."

That narrative is what you saw at the top of your plan page in the amber panel. "Regenerate skeleton" re-runs this single call — nothing else. Useful when the intake changes, or when you want a fresh take on the thesis.

Stage 3 · Candidates

Turn the thesis into specific meals, per slot

One LLM call per (slot, eaters) combination — so ~6 parallel calls for a typical week. Each call gets the skeleton, the intake, the family's recent recipes, and produces 3–5 candidate meals for just its slot. Every candidate comes with a fully-drafted CookCard (ingredients + steps) so cook-night has something to render.

Per-slot specialization

The ADULTS slot pulls from aspirations ("curious about shakshuka"). The KIDS slot pulls from reliableHits ("pasta, quesadillas"). The ADULTS+KIDS slot picks dishes that work for both.

Reuse is the point

If the skeleton says turkey appears in 2 dinners, at least 2 candidates in the dinner slot use it. That's how the grocery list stays tight.

"Regenerate candidates" re-runs all per-slot calls. Non-committed candidates are wiped and replaced; committed ones are preserved so you don't lose your picks. Requires a skeleton to exist.

Skeleton vs candidates

The cheat sheet

Regenerate skeleton

1 LLM call. Cheap + fast (~10s).

Produces: hero ingredients, themes, rationale text.

Shown as: the amber narrative panel at the top of the plan.

When you'd re-run: the week's thesis feels wrong, or you've edited the intake.

Regenerate candidates

N parallel LLM calls (one per slot+eaters combo). Slower (~30–60s).

Produces: 3–5 meals per slot with full CookCard drafts + badges.

Shown as: the menu cards grouped by meal type + eaters.

When you'd re-run: the candidates feel weak, or the skeleton changed.

Order matters. Skeleton first, then candidates. The candidate prompt reads the skeleton to know which heroes to reach for and which themes to honor. Regenerating the skeleton invalidates what the candidates are working off of, so you'll usually follow with a candidate regeneration.

Stage 4 · The menu UI

Browse, commit, un-commit — not a calendar

Candidates are grouped by (slot, eaters). Each section is its own conversation:

Dinner · Adults + kids 1/3 ┌ Turkey + spinach pasta bake ┐ ✓ committed │ One-pan turkey rice bowls │ + │ Sheet-pan harissa chicken │ + └─────────────────────────────┘ Dinner · Adults 0/3 Shakshuka with feta · Orzo with kalamatas · ... ╭─ Breakfast · Kids ──────────────────── 0/2 ─╮ │ Scrambled eggs + toast │ Overnight oats with berries ╰─────────────────────────────────────────────╯

Kid-only sections sit inside a warm amber panel so the kid track is parseable at a glance without reading headers. Adult-only and mixed sections stay neutral. Committed cards flip to emerald. Each card's footer shows up to four hero-ingredient sprites, overlapping like a cast list, so you can see the ingredients at a glance before reading anything.

Stage 5 · Grocery list

Self-assembles from commits — shop once, cook all week

Committing a candidate triggers a rebuild of the plan's grocery list. Every committed meal's ingredients are unioned and deduped by (slug, unit). Uncommitting removes the row (even if it was checked off). Checking an item as purchased persists across rebuilds so you don't lose your place.

✓ bell pepper 3 ☐ black beans 1 can ☐ chicken thighs 2 lb ☐ crushed tomatoes 1 can ☐ eggs 6 ☐ feta ½ cup ☐ kalamata olives ⅓ cup

Each row shows the same ingredient sprite used in the recipe execution flow — visual continuity between planning and cooking. Items yet to be matched to a sprite auto-discover in the background via the same pipeline Mise Place uses.

Stage 6 · Cook night

Open a committed meal in the existing execution guide

When Tuesday rolls around, you open the plan, pick whatever queued meal makes sense tonight, and the app drops you into the existing CookCardView — mise en place, timeline, per-step timers, everything we already had. The planner's job ends at "here's what you're cooking"; execution is untouched.

The key constraint: the planner is additive. The recipe-execution layer that already existed stays exactly as it was — that layer reduces cooking anxiety for the principal user, and we're not diluting it to fit a different workflow.

The hand-off itself — clicking a committed meal → opening CookCardView — is the next small piece to wire after the intake chat. Today's queued meals are browseable but don't yet launch a cook session.

Visual vocabulary

What the colors and shapes mean

  • amber panel — the week's thesis (rationale + themes), or a kid-only section of the menu. Both read as "warm background, guidance inside."
  • emerald — you've committed to this. On cards it fills the commit button and tints the card; on the grocery list it checks off an item.
  • kid chip — the Baby icon + "Kids" label on a section header. Mirrors the amber panel treatment.
  • sprite cluster — the overlapping circles on each card footer. Hero ingredients, at a glance, without reading.
  • badges (e.g. "Kid-reliable", "Under 30 min", "Clears the fridge") — auto-generated from the scoring pass so strong candidates advertise their strengths.

Everything neutral (stone/white) is the default: Adults-only sections, non-committed cards, unchecked grocery rows. Color is reserved for signal.
